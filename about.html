ABOUT US

Stop Antizionism is an educational, analytical, and advocacy initiative committed to exposing antizionism as the third and current era of Jew-hatred, a modern ideological formation that recasts Jews, Israel, and Zionism through the moral vocabulary of our time. Because the appearance of Jew-hatred is an early warning sign of much darker undercurrents aimed at destabilizing democratic societies, we expose antizionism in order to reset our moral compass.

Antizionism does not exist in isolation. At Stop Antizionism, we believe that stopping antizionism is inseparable from defending the core values of America and the West. The appearance of antizionism today is part of a wider ideological movement that seeks to delegitimize liberal democracy, erode national cohesion, and dismantle the moral foundations of the free world.

To confront antizionism is therefore to confront and expose the broader anti-American and anti-Western currents that undermine reason, freedom, accountability, and the principles that sustain open societies.

Drawing on scholarship, historical analysis, and contemporary research, Stop Antizionism demonstrates that we are not witnessing a political debate about Middle Eastern policy, but rather a structural worldview that has mutated from earlier forms of anti-Judaism and racial antisemitism into a new moral narrative in which the Jew, now identified primarily with the State of Israel, is constructed as the supreme violator of today’s highest social virtues.

In this era, antizionism functions as a demonology built through libels that cast Israel and Zionism as racist, colonial, genocidal, or oppressive. These accusations are not simply criticisms; they serve as moral indictments whose purpose is to construct a villain and to provide a framework through which “rescuers” can locate moral meaning, political identity, and ideological purpose.

Antizionism, therefore, operates not as an opinion but as a narrative architecture, one that organizes the world into villains, victims, and rescuers. This antizionist era, like the eras before it, relies on libels as its delivery mechanism — genocide libel, apartheid libel, colonizer libel, racial-supremacy libel, ethnic-cleansing libel, and more.

These libels do not describe reality; they enforce a narrative designed to stigmatize Jews and the Jewish state while legitimizing hostility, exclusion, and in some cases, political violence.

At Stop Antizionism, our mission is to illuminate the continuity between these eras so that the Jewish community and its allies can grasp the full arc of Jew-hatred and confront the present moment with clarity, recognizing antizionism for what it is: the latest mutation of antisemitism and a threat to civilization.

Our Mission

As a modern ideological system of Jew-hatred continues to reshape public discourse, Stop Antizionism works to expose its architecture and equip communities with the knowledge, tools, and strategies needed to confront it. Our mission is to illuminate, analyze, and strategically counter this evolving Jew-hate movement.

Our Mission is to

  • Expose antizionism as a coherent ideological system, not a collection of political critiques. The core claim of the vision is that antizionism is Jew-hatred in contemporary form, and the organization exists to make that reality universally recognized.
  • Reveal antizionism as part of a broader ideological attack on the West. The organization frames antizionism not only as a danger to Jews, but as a threat to liberal democracy, Western values, constitutional order, free inquiry, and moral agency. In other words: defending Jews and defending the West are inseparable goals.
  • Teach people to decode antizionism as a narrative system, not a political debate.

    • Stop Antizionism trains people to see:

  • How antizionism cloaks itself in the language of justice and liberation
  • How to recognize the narrative patterns, the villainization, the moral inversions, the libels, and the disinformation that shape contemporary attitudes toward Jews and Israel.
  • How it constructs villain/victim/rescuer roles
  • How it redefines moral categories
  • How it embeds itself in “progressive” academic, cultural, and civic frameworks
  • How it corrodes democratic norms and freedom
  • Provide tools and education to counter this narrative warfare. The organization operationalizes its insights into:
  • Educational tools to help transform public discourse by shifting from defensive “advocacy” toward strategic naming, boundary-setting, and narrative correction.
  • Training for teachers and institutions
  • Intellectual frameworks
  • Strategic approaches to countering antizionist ideology

Our mandate is to build a society that clearly recognizes antizionism as the modern mutation of historical Jew-hatreds—structurally identical, merely expressed in updated moral language. We aim to teach the public to see the recurring patterns of this ideology; expose its Soviet and post-colonial origins; and trace its spread through academia, media, culture, and civic institutions.

By equipping communities with the intellectual tools to resist manipulative narratives and stop the normalization of anti-Zionist demonology, our mission advances a transformation in public discourse—from defensive advocacy to strategic naming, boundary-setting, and narrative correction—so that truth, clarity, and moral courage prevail.

  • Policy makers and legal professionals, who must understand the structural nature of anti-Zionism to craft effective responses.
  • Community leaders, activists, and the general public, who need a coherent language to describe and confront this emerging civilizational threat.
  • Forge coalitions and strategic allyship to publicly name antizionism as Jew-hatred and racism, and to empower collective resistance to this ideological movement.
  • Educators, who require frameworks to teach today's students how antizionist narratives operate.
  • Students, who face hostile campus climates shaped by ideological disinformation.
  • Journalists and cultural institutions, who seek clarity on the distinctions between criticism, propaganda, and hate.
