ABOUT STOP ANTIZIONISM

WHO WE ARE

STOP Antizionism is an educational and advocacy organization dedicated to identifying, exposing, and confronting antizionism as the contemporary form of Jew-hatred. Our mission begins with a simple principle: before antizionism can be effectively confronted, it must first be recognized and named.

At the center of our work is the STOP AZ Global Declaration, the first declaration of its kind to publicly identify antizionism as today’s most lethal form of Jew-hatred. Signed by more than 55 organizations and 5,000 individuals, the Declaration provides a framework for understanding how antizionism operates and offers a roadmap for policymakers, legislators, educators, school administrators, and civic leaders confronting antizionism in city halls, on college campuses, in schools, libraries, and other public institutions.

STOP Antizionism goes beyond raising awareness. Through education, research, leadership training, public advocacy, and coalition building, we equip individuals and institutions with the language and tools necessary to recognize antizionism, understand its historical and ideological roots, and respond to it effectively.

Our work is grounded in the conviction that antizionism cannot be understood simply as a debate over Israeli policy or even as opposition to Zionism. It targets the Jewish collective by demonizing the Jewish nation-state and subjecting it to accusations and standards that have historically been used to stigmatize Jews themselves.

STOP Antizionism is building a global movement committed to changing how antizionism is understood and confronted. We work with educators, students, policymakers, community leaders, organizations, and ordinary citizens who believe that combating Jew-hatred requires the courage to recognize its contemporary form.

Our message is simple: Name it. Understand it. Stop it.

DR. NAYA LEKHT

Dr. Naya Lekht is a scholar, educator, author, and leading voice on contemporary antizionism and Jew-hatred. She is the co-founder of STOP Antizionism, an educational and advocacy organization dedicated to identifying and confronting antizionism as the contemporary form of Jew-hatred.

Born in the former Soviet Union and raised in the United States, Dr. Lekht brings both personal experience and scholarly expertise to her study of Soviet and contemporary antizionism. She holds a Ph.D. in Russian Literature from UCLA, where her doctoral research examined Holocaust literature in the Soviet Union, with particular attention to Babi Yar, censorship, and Soviet Jewish memory.
